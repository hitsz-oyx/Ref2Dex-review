Yes. I think this is a real semantic problem in the current OI-Cm training, and it is more fundamental than a decoder issue.

Right now a training sample is defined as

$$
(t,\;t+\text{stride}),
$$

with

$$
\Delta H
=
H_{t+\text{stride}}-H_t,
\qquad
\Delta O
=
O_{t+\text{stride}}-O_t.
$$

The repository currently samples GRAB strides \(1\ldots10\) and Inspire even strides \(2\ldots20\), while optionally giving `delta_time_s` to the model.

The problem is that an endpoint displacement is **not necessarily one action**.

If a pair spans

$$
\text{approach}
\rightarrow
\text{contact}
\rightarrow
\text{grasp}
\rightarrow
\text{lift},
$$

then Cm is being told:

$$
\boxed{
\text{all of this cumulative displacement is one local interaction action}.
}
$$

That breaks the causal meaning we want for Cm.

## The most important fix: redefine Cm as a local transition model

I would change the semantics to:

$$
\boxed{
C_{m,t}=F(O_t,H_t,\Delta H_{t\rightarrow t+\delta})
}
$$

where \(\delta\) corresponds to a **small physical control interval**, rather than an arbitrary long future horizon.

Then predict:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta O_{t\rightarrow t+\delta}.
}
$$

In other words, Cm should answer:

> Given what the hand is doing **now**, what does the object do **next**?

Not:

> Given where the hand eventually ends up several frames later, where does the object eventually end up?

That is the world-model interpretation we actually need for RL.

---

# I would not solve it merely by reducing `max_stride`

That is the easiest patch, but it is not quite enough.

Suppose you pick `stride=2`. Near a contact transition, those two frames can still cross:

$$
\text{no contact}\rightarrow\text{contact}.
$$

Or:

$$
\text{contact}\rightarrow\text{lift}.
$$

So the correct concept is not just **short stride**. It is:

$$
\boxed{\text{short + phase-consistent transition}.}
$$

I would rebuild the pair sampler around that.

---

## 1. Define an immediate horizon

Choose a physical horizon based on the control frequency, not an arbitrary frame count.

For example, conceptually:

$$
\Delta t_{\rm Cm}\approx 30\text{–}100\text{ ms}
$$

depending on the source data frequency and the eventual simulator control step.

I would choose it so that in most samples:

* fingers move meaningfully;
* but contact topology normally does not go through several distinct phases;
* object motion is still locally attributable to that action.

Because GRAB and Inspire have different frame rates, specify this in **seconds**, not source-specific frame indices:

$$
\delta_s
\approx
\Delta t_{\rm target}\cdot fps_s.
$$

The dataset already carries

$$
\texttt{delta\_time\_s}=\frac{\text{stride}}{\text{effective\_fps}},
$$

so the infrastructure is already close to supporting this.

---

# 2. Do not let a sample cross interaction phases unnecessarily

Offline, derive a coarse phase/contact state for every frame.

It does not have to be a perfect semantic annotation. Something simple is enough:

$$
c_t=
\begin{cases}
0 &: d_{\min}>d_{\rm near}\\
1 &: \text{near/contact}\\
2 &: \text{object being manipulated}
\end{cases}
$$

or even use continuous descriptors:

$$
d_{\min}(t),
$$

$$
N_{\rm contact}(t),
$$

$$
\|\Delta O_t\|.
$$

Then when constructing a local pair, prefer windows where the interaction regime is stable.

For example:

$$
c_t=c_{t+\delta}
$$

for ordinary local dynamics samples.

But I would **not discard transition samples entirely**.

Contact establishment itself is important.

Instead explicitly label them:

$$
\text{approach}\rightarrow\text{contact},
$$

$$
\text{contact}\rightarrow\text{manipulation},
$$

$$
\text{contact}\rightarrow\text{release}.
$$

Then the model knows that these are **transition events**, not ordinary steady interaction.

This gives much cleaner supervision.

---

# 3. Most importantly: use incremental motion, not only endpoint displacement

Suppose the original stride is 10:

$$
H_t,\ H_{t+1},\ldots,H_{t+10}.
$$

Current Cm effectively gets:

$$
H_{t+10}-H_t.
$$

That loses the entire trajectory shape.

These two trajectories can have exactly the same endpoint:

$$
A:
\quad
\text{approach}\rightarrow\text{push}
$$

and

$$
B:
\quad
\text{move sideways}\rightarrow\text{approach},
$$

yet

$$
H_{t+10}^{A}-H_t
\approx
H_{t+10}^{B}-H_t.
$$

Their physical effects may be completely different.

If we want a larger temporal receptive field, represent it as a sequence:

$$
\Delta H_{t:t+K}
=
[
H_{t+1}-H_t,\,
H_{t+2}-H_{t+1},\ldots
].
$$

Then use a small temporal encoder:

$$
E_{\rm temporal}
(
\Delta H_t,\ldots,\Delta H_{t+K-1}
)
\rightarrow z_a.
$$

But I would **not do this for the first correction**.

For RL, the cleanest Cm is one-step/local.

---

# 4. Separate “action” from “intention”

I think this is where the original multi-stride design went wrong conceptually.

Long strides are useful because they tell us:

> Where is the manipulation going?

But that's **intent**, not an instantaneous action.

We should have two separate representations.

### Local action

$$
a_t^{local}
=
\Delta H_{t\rightarrow t+\delta}.
$$

This feeds OI-Cm:

$$
(s_t,a_t^{local})
\rightarrow
C_{m,t}^{local}
\rightarrow
\Delta O_{t\rightarrow t+\delta}.
$$

### Longer-term reference / intent

For example:

$$
g_t=
[
O_{t+H}^{ref},
\text{future contact goal},
\text{future reference trajectory}
].
$$

This goes into the RL policy or high-level planner.

Do **not** force Cm to encode both.

That gives us:

$$
\boxed{
\text{reference future tells us what we want}
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\text{Cm tells us what the current action will do}.
}
$$

That is much cleaner.

---

# 5. I would keep multi-horizon supervision, but only as an auxiliary task

We don't necessarily need to throw away the long-stride data.

Use the short horizon as the **primary Cm definition**:

$$
L_{\rm local}
=
L(
\hat{\Delta O}_{t,t+\delta},
\Delta O_{t,t+\delta}
).
$$

Then optionally add auxiliary heads:

$$
\hat{\Delta O}^{100ms},
\quad
\hat{\Delta O}^{200ms},
$$

conditioned explicitly on horizon.

But don't mix them all into one undifferentiated endpoint target.

Conceptually:

$$
C_m^{local}
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
F_{1}(C_m) & \Delta t=33ms\\
F_{2}(C_m) & \Delta t=66ms\\
F_{3}(C_m) & \Delta t=100ms
\end{cases}
$$

or one time-conditioned effect decoder:

$$
F(C_m,\Delta t).
$$

The repository already has an optional `delta_time_s` condition, so this would be a natural evolution rather than a totally new architecture.

---

# 6. But `delta_time_s` alone does not solve the current problem

This is important.

The current architecture can tell the model:

$$
\Delta t=0.3s.
$$

That does not tell it whether those 0.3 seconds contain:

$$
\text{only approach}
$$

or

$$
\text{approach + contact + lift}.
$$

Time conditioning fixes **scale ambiguity**.

It does not fix **causal phase mixing**.

So I would not simply say:

> keep strides 1–20 and add stronger time conditioning.

I think the training-pair semantics themselves need to change.

---

# 7. I would also change the target from displacement to velocity/twist where useful

For the hand, instead of only:

$$
\Delta p_h,
$$

we can use:

$$
v_h
=
\frac{\Delta p_h}{\Delta t}.
$$

For rigid object effect, ideally predict local twist:

$$
\xi_o
=
(v_o,\omega_o).
$$

Then over a small interval:

$$
\Delta T_o
=
\exp(\xi_o\Delta t).
$$

This makes different source frame rates much easier to combine.

But again, normalization does not solve phase mixing. It should come **after** shortening/localizing the transition.

---

# 8. Contact establishment needs special treatment

There is one subtle case.

Suppose at time \(t\):

$$
d=2.5\text{ cm},
$$

and the hand action moves inward so that at \(t+\delta\):

$$
d=0.5\text{ cm}.
$$

If OI-Cm only constructs edges from current points within 2 cm, it sees no interaction evidence at time \(t\).

Yet that action is about to establish contact.

This is where I would modify the architecture slightly:

Keep the **2 cm region for fine interaction**, but add a coarse proximity context, perhaps:

$$
2\text{–}5\text{ cm}.
$$

Not full detailed interaction features—just enough to represent:

$$
\text{approaching object}.
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
0\text{–}2\text{ cm}: fine contact interaction
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
2\text{–}5\text{ cm}: coarse approach context
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
>5\text{ cm}: normally no interaction.
}
$$

This lets the world model learn the transition

$$
\text{approach}\rightarrow\text{contact}
$$

without redefining every 5 cm neighbor as a contact.

---

# 9. What I would do to the current OI-Cm dataset

Concretely, I would make a new dataset version rather than silently changing V1.3.

For each frame \(t\):

1. Compute a small physical target horizon \(\delta\).
2. Build:

$$
\Delta H_t=H_{t+\delta}-H_t.
$$

3. Build:

$$
\Delta O_t=O_{t+\delta}-O_t.
$$

4. Record:

$$
\Delta t,
\quad
d_{\min,t},
\quad
d_{\min,t+\delta},
$$

and contact-state transition.

5. Categorize samples:

$$
\texttt{free},
\quad
\texttt{approach},
\quad
\texttt{contact},
\quad
\texttt{release}.
$$

6. Balance these categories during sampling.

That last step matters.

If we just shorten stride, the dataset can become dominated by:

$$
\Delta O\approx0.
$$

Then the network learns a trivial zero-effect model.

So we need balanced sampling between:

* no-effect;
* contact establishment;
* active manipulation;
* contact loss.

---

# 10. I would retrain OI-Cm after this change

Unfortunately I don't think this can be fixed only at inference.

The current token representation learned from endpoint flow whose semantic meaning can include multiple phases.

If we now expect it to mean:

$$
\text{local action at this instant},
$$

that is a different learning problem.

So I would preserve the old V1.3 checkpoint for comparison, but train a new version with local-time semantics.

And the decisive comparison would not be only object EPE.

I would test:

$$
(s_t,a_t)
\rightarrow
\Delta O_{t+\delta}
$$

for true one-step transitions, especially:

* no-contact action;
* approaching contact;
* sustained contact;
* manipulation;
* release.

That is exactly the data distribution our RL policy will create.

---

## My preferred final definition of Cm

I would write the contract very explicitly:

$$
\boxed{
C_m(s_t,a_t)
=
\text{the local object-grounded interaction induced by one short action}.
}
$$

Not:

$$
C_m(s_t,a_{t:t+H})
=
\text{the cumulative manipulation over a long future interval}.
$$

Then long-term manipulation becomes:

$$
C_{m,t},
C_{m,t+1},
C_{m,t+2},
\dots
$$

a **sequence of local interactions**.

That is exactly how a world model should work.

It also solves several problems we've been discussing at once:

* Cm predictions become compatible with RL control frequency;
* approach/contact/lift are no longer collapsed into one “action”;
* false object effects become easier to diagnose;
* Cm can be updated from real simulator transitions naturally;
* cross-hand transfer becomes better defined because we compare local physical interactions rather than entire long trajectories;
* we no longer need CmDecoder to reconstruct a large cumulative motion from one latent.

So I would consider this more than a stride hyperparameter adjustment. **It is a correction to the temporal semantics of OI-Cm, and I think it should be made before we invest heavily in using Cm as an RL world-model prior.**
