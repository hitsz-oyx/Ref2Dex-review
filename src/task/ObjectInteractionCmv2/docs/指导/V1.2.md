Yes. I would change the contact construction for the new OI-Cm, but **not by simply feeding GT next-frame contacts into the current frame**.

The clean solution is:

$$
\boxed{\text{stride}=1\text{ at 30 Hz}
+\text{action-swept contact neighborhood}
+\text{soft 2 cm boundary}}
$$

This also gives us a much cleaner PPO critic.

## 1. Stride = 1 is the right choice

At 30 Hz,

$$
\Delta t \approx 33.3\text{ ms}.
$$

That substantially reduces temporal aliasing compared with larger strides. The repository's downstream CmDecoder work already uses 30 Hz / stride 1, while OI-Cm's current interaction definition still uses KNN32 and a hard 2 cm radius.

So I would make the new OI-Cm training contract strictly:

$$
(t,t+1)
$$

at 30 Hz.

But stride 1 **does not solve the 2 cm discontinuity by itself**.

Imagine:

$$
d_t=20.5\text{ mm},
$$

and one frame later:

$$
d_{t+1}=14\text{ mm}.
$$

Under the current rule the interaction goes from

$$
0\rightarrow\text{fully active}
$$

in one frame.

That's exactly the discontinuity you're worried about.

---

# 2. Should we use points that are within 2 cm at the next frame?

**Yes, but only if “next frame” is computed from the proposed hand action.**

This distinction is critical.

OI-Cm already receives hand point flow:

$$
\Delta p_j^h.
$$

Therefore we know, from the candidate action,

$$
\tilde p_{j,t+1}^h
=
p_{j,t}^h+\Delta p_{j,t}^h.
$$

Using that is causal.

We are not leaking the unknown physical outcome. We are simply asking:

> Given the hand motion I am proposing, which hand points will approach the current object surface during this control step?

What I would **not** use is the GT future object position:

$$
p_{i,t+1}^{o,GT}.
$$

Because that is exactly the effect OI-Cm is supposed to predict.

So:

$$
\boxed{
P_h^{t+1,\mathrm{proposed}}
\text{ is allowed;}
\quad
P_o^{t+1,\mathrm{GT}}
\text{ is not.}
}
$$

---

# 3. I would go one step better than current + next

Use the entire **swept hand segment**.

For an object point \(p_i^o\) and hand point \(p_j^h\), define

$$
r_{ij}=p_j^h-p_i^o
$$

and proposed motion

$$
v_j=\Delta p_j^h.
$$

The hand point follows approximately

$$
p_j(\alpha)=p_j^h+\alpha v_j,
\qquad
0\leq\alpha\leq1.
$$

Compute the closest point along that segment:

$$
\alpha^\star
=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
-\frac{r_{ij}^{T}v_j}
{\|v_j\|^2+\epsilon},
0,1
\right).
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
d_{ij}^{sweep}
=
\|r_{ij}+\alpha^\star v_j\|.
}
$$

This is better than checking only

$$
d_t
$$

and

$$
d_{t+1}.
$$

A hand point could theoretically pass close to the object and leave again inside the 33 ms interval. The swept distance captures that.

So the interaction edge means:

> **Will this proposed hand-point motion enter the interaction neighborhood during this step?**

That is exactly the semantics an action-conditioned world model should have.

---

# 4. I would also remove the hard 2 cm binary boundary

I would preserve **2 cm as the physical core radius**, but not as a binary neural-network switch.

Current:

$$
m_{ij}
=
\mathbf 1[d_{ij}<0.02].
$$

I would instead build candidate edges in a slightly larger halo, perhaps

$$
r_{\rm outer}=0.03\text{ m},
$$

and assign a smooth weight.

For example:

$$
w(d)=
\begin{cases}
1,&d\leq0.02\\[1mm]
\frac12
\left[
1+\cos
\left(
\pi\frac{d-0.02}{0.01}
\right)
\right],
&0.02<d<0.03\\[2mm]
0,&d\geq0.03.
\end{cases}
$$

Use

$$
d=d^{sweep}_{ij}.
$$

Then:

* 15 mm → full interaction;
* 20 mm → full/core interaction;
* 22 mm → almost active;
* 27 mm → weak pre-contact context;
* 30 mm → zero.

So the semantic statement remains:

$$
\boxed{\text{2 cm is the actual interaction core}}
$$

while the network gets a smooth **pre-contact halo**.

I prefer this to simply changing the threshold from 2 cm to 3 or 5 cm.

---

# 5. The actual edge feature I would use

For every candidate object-hand edge:

$$
x_{ij}=
[
r_{ij},
d_t,
d_{end},
d_{sweep},
w_{ij},
n_i^o,
n_j^h,
n_i^o\cdot n_j^h,
\Delta p_j^h,
v_{normal},
v_{tangent}
].
$$

Where:

$$
d_{end}
=
\|
p_j^h+\Delta p_j^h-p_i^o
\|,
$$

and

$$
v_{normal}
=
\Delta p_j^h\cdot n_i^o,
$$

$$
v_{tangent}
=
\Delta p_j^h-v_{normal}n_i^o.
$$

Then multiply/condition the interaction message with

$$
w_{ij}.
$$

Now the model can distinguish:

* already touching;
* approaching;
* moving away;
* sliding tangentially;
* about to enter interaction range.

That is much richer than merely “distance < 2 cm.”

---

# 6. This also fixes the sudden appearance of many points

This is actually more important than the exact threshold.

Right now the active set itself changes discretely:

$$
\mathcal A_t
\neq
\mathcal A_{t+1}.
$$

Instead I would make the **object point set fixed**.

For each of the 1024 object points, always retain its K nearest hand candidates, for example K=32.

Then each edge has a continuous weight:

$$
w_{ij}\in[0,1].
$$

Thus an object point doesn't suddenly “appear in the network.”

It is always there:

$$
p_i^o
\rightarrow
g_i.
$$

Only its interaction strength changes smoothly:

$$
0.01
\rightarrow0.08
\rightarrow0.35
\rightarrow0.9.
$$

This is much better for temporal consistency.

So my preferred V2 structure is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{fixed object points}
+
\text{fixed KNN candidate edges}
+
\text{soft swept interaction weights}.
}
$$

Not a dynamically changing active point set.

---

# 7. Do we still need temporal memory?

Probably not initially.

With

* stride 1,
* swept-action geometry,
* smooth weighting,

most of the artificial temporal discontinuity disappears.

I would first test a stateless model:

$$
F(s_t,a_t)\rightarrow e_t.
$$

Only if we still see oscillation would I add

$$
C_{m,t-1}
$$

or a GRU/temporal transformer.

I would avoid recurrence until necessary because we want Cm to remain a clean local forward model.

---

# 8. Now, what should the PPO critic actually look like?

This point matters a lot.

**OI-Cm itself should not be the PPO critic.**

OI-Cm is:

$$
F_{Cm}(s,a)
\rightarrow
(z,\hat e).
$$

The PPO critic remains:

$$
V_\phi(\cdot)\rightarrow\mathbb R.
$$

Cm provides physics-aware features to it.

For the first serious version I would use the following architecture.

---

## Base action

Frozen DExplore/tracking policy:

$$
a_t^b
=
\pi_b(o_t).
$$

Convert that to candidate hand point flow:

$$
\Delta P_{h,t}^b
=
FK(s_t,a_t^b)-P_{h,t}.
$$

Then new OI-Cm evaluates the **base action**:

$$
(z_t^b,\hat e_t^b,c_t)
=
F_{Cm}^{target}
(
P_o^t,
P_h^t,
\Delta P_{h,t}^b
).
$$

Here:

* \(z_t^b\): interaction representation/tokens;
* \(\hat e_t^b\): predicted object effect;
* \(c_t\): confidence.

Reference gives:

$$
e_t^\star.
$$

Then define effect discrepancy:

$$
\Delta e_t
=
e_t^\star-\hat e_t^b.
$$

---

# 9. Actor

I would eventually give the actor:

$$
\boxed{
x_t^\pi=
[
o_t,
a_t^b,
\Psi(z_t^b),
\Delta e_t,
c_t
].
}
$$

Then:

$$
\delta a_t
\sim
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x_t^\pi).
$$

And:

$$
a_t
=
a_t^b
+
S\delta a_t.
$$

Where \(S\) is our small residual authority.

This means Cm has an active role:

> “The DExplore action is predicted to produce effect X; the reference wants effect Y; generate a small robot-specific correction.”

No inverse CmDecoder is required.

# 10. The PPO critic should use essentially the same information, but it remains a **state-value critic**

I would define:

$$
\boxed{
V_\phi(x_t^V)
}
$$

with

$$
x_t^V=
[
o_t^{priv},
a_t^b,
\Psi(z_t^b),
\Phi(e_t^\star),
\Phi(\hat e_t^b),
\Phi(e_t^\star-\hat e_t^b),
c_t
].
$$

Then:

$$
V_\phi
=
MLP(
[
h_{state},
h_{Cm},
h_{effect}
]).
$$

More concretely:

$$
h_{state}
=
MLP_s(o_t^{priv})
$$

$$
h_{Cm}
=
SetEncoder
(
\{
[z_k,a_k,w_k]
\}_{k=1}^K
)
$$

$$
h_{effect}
=
MLP_e(
[
e_t^\star,
\hat e_t^b,
e_t^\star-\hat e_t^b,
c_t
]
)
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
V_t=
MLP_V(
[h_{state},h_{Cm},h_{effect}]
).
}
$$

I would **not flatten anonymous slot tokens by index** if we redesign OI-Cm. Use permutation-invariant attention/pooling over grounded contact tokens.

# 11. Why evaluate the base action in the PPO critic rather than the sampled residual action?

Because ordinary PPO's critic is:

$$
V(s_t),
$$

not

$$
Q(s_t,a_t).
$$

The value baseline should be computable **before sampling the stochastic action**.

So using

$$
F_{Cm}(s_t,a_t^b)
$$

is clean because \(a_t^b\) is deterministic given state/reference.

Then Cm-derived features are part of the state representation.

If we instead put

$$
F_{Cm}(s_t,a_t^b+\delta a_t)
$$

into the standard PPO critic, then the critic becomes action-conditioned:

$$
Q(s,a),
$$

which changes the algorithm.

That is not inherently wrong, but it is no longer the normal PPO value baseline.

---

# 12. If we want Cm to evaluate the **actual residual candidate**, add a second head

This is probably our later architecture.

After PPO samples

$$
\delta a_t,
$$

construct:

$$
a_t^{cand}
=
a_t^b+S\delta a_t.
$$

Run:

$$
(z_t^{cand},\hat e_t^{cand})
=
F_{Cm}(s_t,a_t^{cand}).
$$

Then have an auxiliary action-value/effect head:

$$
\boxed{
Q_\psi
(
s_t,
\delta a_t,
z_t^{cand},
e_t^\star-\hat e_t^{cand}
)
}
$$

trained toward something like

$$
r_t+\gamma V(s_{t+1}).
$$

Then we have:

### PPO critic

$$
V_\phi(s_t,C_m^{base})
$$

for stable advantage estimation.

### Auxiliary action critic

$$
Q_\psi(s_t,\delta a_t,C_m^{cand})
$$

for candidate-action evaluation.

This is much cleaner than forcing one network to perform both roles.

Eventually, this \(Q\) head can rank multiple residual candidates.

---

# 13. How should Cm be fine-tuned during RL?

The new stride-1 representation actually makes this easier.

Every simulator step directly gives:

$$
(P_h^t,P_o^t,a_t,P_h^{t+1},P_o^{t+1}).
$$

Therefore:

$$
\Delta P_h^{GT}=P_h^{t+1}-P_h^t
$$

and

$$
\Delta P_o^{GT}=P_o^{t+1}-P_o^t.
$$

Train an online Cm on those transitions.

But PPO sees a slowly updated target Cm:

$$
F_{Cm}^{target}.
$$

So:

$$
\text{PPO rollout}
\rightarrow
\text{replay data}
\rightarrow
F_{Cm}^{online}
$$

and periodically:

$$
\theta_{target}
\leftarrow
(1-\tau)\theta_{target}
+
\tau\theta_{online}.
$$

That keeps the critic reasonably stationary.

---

## My concrete recommendation for OI-Cm V2

I would implement the input contract as:

$$
\boxed{
(P_o^t,N_o^t,P_h^t,N_h^t,\Delta P_h^{candidate})
}
$$

at **30 Hz / stride 1**.

For each object point:

1. KNN candidates within an outer 3 cm halo;
2. calculate current distance;
3. calculate action-advected end distance;
4. calculate swept-segment minimum distance;
5. use 2 cm as the core radius;
6. smoothly taper interaction weight between 2 and 3 cm;
7. aggregate the weighted edge messages.

No GT \(P_o^{t+1}\) enters the input.

That gives us:

$$
\boxed{
\text{causal}
+
\text{action-conditioned}
+
\text{temporally smooth}
+
\text{still contact-local}.
}
$$

And for PPO I would initially use:

$$
\boxed{
V_\phi
(
o_t,
a_t^{base},
C_m(s_t,a_t^{base}),
e_t^\star-\hat e_t^{base}
)
}
$$

as the critic, while the residual actor gradually receives the same effect-discrepancy features once the basic residual learning is stable.

So yes: **include “next-frame proximity,” but derive it from the proposed hand flow, not from GT future object state.** And I would implement it as a swept, softly weighted contact field rather than simply unioning two hard 2 cm masks.
