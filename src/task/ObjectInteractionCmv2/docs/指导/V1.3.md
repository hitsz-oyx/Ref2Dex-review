Yes. After looking at the current `feature/objectinteractioncmv2-v1.0.2`, I would **keep the hard 2 cm swept-contact design**, but I would change several things in the network before committing to a large GRAB/DexYCB training run.

The core issue is no longer contact selection. It is **how much spatial interaction structure survives from the local contacts to the final SE(3) prediction**.

### 1. Replace the current mean-based effect fusion

This is the most important change.

The current model forms the direct representation approximately as

$$
G=\operatorname{mean}_i g_i,
\qquad
I=\frac{\sum_{i\in active}c_i}{|active|},
$$

and the token representation as

$$
Z=\operatorname{mean}_k z_k.
$$

Then

$$
h=\operatorname{MLP}([G,I,Z])
\rightarrow
\Delta\xi.
$$

That is exactly what the current implementation does: global mean geometry, average active contact, then `tokens.mean(1)` before the effect head.

This is too aggressive for predicting rotation.

Two interactions could have almost identical average contact features but occur on opposite sides of the object:

$$
\text{contact left}
\qquad \text{vs}\qquad
\text{contact right}.
$$

Their translations may look similar, while their induced rotations can be opposite.

Your architecture document actually describes the better design: grounded tokens with anchors/normals followed by hybrid part–token fusion/cross-attention.

I would implement that literally.

For each token define

$$
u_k=
\operatorname{MLP}
[
z_k,\;
a_k,\;
n_k,\;
m_k
],
$$

where

* \(z_k\): interaction feature;
* \(a_k\): object-frame anchor;
* \(n_k\): anchor normal;
* \(m_k\): contact mass/coverage.

Construct a part/global query

$$
q=
\operatorname{MLP}([G,I]),
$$

then:

$$
q'
=
\operatorname{CrossAttention}
(q,\{u_k\}).
$$

Finally:

$$
\Delta\xi_{\rm root}
=
f_{\rm SE3}(q').
$$

This lets the effect head explicitly know **where** interaction happened.

That would be my highest-priority modification.

---

### 2. Fix the token assignment mechanism

The current `GroundedContactTokens` has a subtle problem.

It produces token logits and then softmaxes **over object points separately for every token**:

$$
w_{ki}
=
\operatorname{softmax}_{i}(l_{ki}).
$$

This means every token independently selects from essentially the same active contact set. There is no strong competition saying:

> this contact belongs primarily to token 3 rather than token 7.

Consequently, token duplication/collapse is quite possible.

There's another consequence: whenever there is at least one active point, practically every token receives normalized mass, so the current

$$
\texttt{token\_mask}
=
\mathbf 1[\sum_iw_{ki}>0]
$$

doesn't really tell you which tokens are meaningful.

I would instead use **point-to-token competitive assignment**:

$$
A_{ik}
=
\operatorname{softmax}_{k}(l_{ik}).
$$

Then

$$
m_k=\sum_i A_{ik},
$$

and

$$
z_k=
\frac{\sum_iA_{ik}c_i}
{m_k+\epsilon}.
$$

Likewise,

$$
a_k=
\frac{\sum_iA_{ik}p_i}
{m_k+\epsilon}.
$$

Now \(m_k\) is a real token mass and you can define

$$
\text{token\_mask}_k
=
[m_k>\epsilon].
$$

This gives the token representation substantially clearer semantics.

I would also make token assignment explicitly spatial:

$$
l_{ik}
=
f([c_i,p_i,n_i]),
$$

rather than deriving assignment only from \(c_i\).

---

### 3. Disable the residual-flow head for the first rigid-object model

This one I would change immediately.

The current residual head outputs:

$$
r\in\mathbb R^3
$$

and then expands that same vector to every object point:

$$
r_i=r.
$$

But a constant per-point residual flow is mathematically indistinguishable from rigid translation.

You currently have:

$$
\Delta p_i
=
\underbrace{
(Rp_i+t)-p_i
}_{\text{structured}}
+
\underbrace{r}_{\text{residual}}.
$$

So:

$$
t+r
$$

is observationally equivalent to another translation.

The decomposition is therefore not identifiable.

And the active GRAB config currently enables this with:

```text
use_residual: true
```

For rigid GRAB/DexYCB training I would set:

$$
\boxed{\texttt{use\_residual=false}}.
$$

Your earlier diagnostics already showed that these objects are essentially rigid. Let the network prove it can predict

$$
\Delta SE(3)
$$

properly.

If a residual is needed later, make it a true nonrigid residual and explicitly project out the best-fitting rigid component.

---

### 4. Directly supervise SE(3), not only reconstructed point flow

The current loss is mainly:

$$
L_{\rm flow}
=
\operatorname{SmoothL1}
(
\hat{\Delta P}_o,
\Delta P_o^{GT}
).
$$

The root SE(3) prediction is learned indirectly through that flow reconstruction.

Since GRAB/DexYCB rigid-object pose is available, I would supervise the structured quantity directly.

Obtain:

$$
T_{t\rightarrow t+1}^{GT}.
$$

Then use:

$$
L_t=
\|\hat t-t^{GT}\|_1
$$

and a rotation/geodesic loss such as

$$
L_R
=
\operatorname{acos}
\left(
\frac{
\operatorname{tr}(
R_{GT}^{T}\hat R
)-1
}{2}
\right).
$$

Keep point-flow reconstruction as an auxiliary:

$$
L=
\lambda_tL_t
+
\lambda_RL_R
+
\lambda_fL_{\rm flow}
+
\lambda_eL_{\rm effect}.
$$

This gives much clearer supervision to the exact quantity you ultimately want to expose to RL:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta t,\Delta R.
}
$$

---

### 5. Change the `p_effect` label

Currently:

$$
\text{has\_effect}
=
\mathbf 1[
\max_i\|\Delta p_i^{GT}\|>10^{-6}
].
$$

That threshold is essentially “numerically nonzero.”

For real motion-capture data, almost everything can become positive because of annotation noise/jitter.

I would derive the effect/no-effect boundary from the actual stationary-motion distribution rather than \(10^{-6}\).

For example, estimate the noise floor from nominally static frames and choose a threshold from that distribution.

I would not hard-code a number until you've measured GRAB/DexYCB noise.

This matters because `p_effect` could become a very useful signal for the residual actor later.

---

### 6. Add explicit feature scaling

Current swept features mix quantities with very different numerical ranges:

$$
r,\;
d_t,\;
d_{end},\;
d_{sweep},
\;
n_o,\;
n_h,\;
\Delta h,\;
v_n,\;
v_t,\;
\Delta t.
$$

You are mixing meters, unit normals, millimeter-scale displacements, and seconds directly.

Before multi-dataset training, I would explicitly nondimensionalize them.

For example:

$$
\bar r=\frac{r}{0.02},
\qquad
\bar d=\frac{d}{0.02},
$$

because 2 cm is your interaction scale.

Likewise, either use

$$
v_h=\frac{\Delta h}{\Delta t}
$$

or provide both displacement and normalized velocity.

For global object geometry, use a characteristic object scale \(s_o\):

$$
\bar p_o=\frac{p_o}{s_o},
$$

but also pass \(s_o\) to the global branch so physical size is not discarded.

This will matter much more once GRAB and DexYCB are mixed.

---

## 7. Keep internal tokens rich, but expose smaller Cm tokens to RL

The current configuration is:

$$
16 \times 128
$$

tokens.

That is fine internally.

But I would not feed all

$$
2048
$$

dimensions directly into a residual actor.

Separate:

$$
z_k^{internal}\in\mathbb R^{128}
$$

from

$$
z_k^{Cm}
=
Wz_k^{internal}
\in\mathbb R^{32\text{–}64}.
$$

The effect head can continue using the rich 128-D tokens.

The public Cm representation used by RL can be something like:

$$
16\times32.
$$

That gives a clean distinction:

$$
\boxed{
\text{internal physics representation}
\neq
\text{cross-embodiment control interface}.
}
$$

This also makes your paper story stronger.

# Architecture I would use

For rigid-object V2:

$$
(P_o,N_o)
\rightarrow
\text{geometry encoder}
\rightarrow
g_i
$$

and

$$
(P_h,N_h,\Delta P_h)
\rightarrow
\text{swept hard-2cm edges}
\rightarrow
c_i.
$$

Then two branches.

### Global/direct branch

$$
G=
\operatorname{AttentionPool}(\{g_i\})
$$

rather than only `mean`.

And

$$
I=
\operatorname{AttentionPool}(\{c_i:i\in active\}).
$$

Then:

$$
q_0=\operatorname{MLP}([G,I]).
$$

### Grounded token branch

Competitive assignment:

$$
(c_i,p_i,n_i)
\rightarrow
\{z_k,a_k,n_k,m_k\}_{k=1}^{K}.
$$

Enrich:

$$
u_k=
\operatorname{MLP}
([z_k,a_k,n_k,\log(m_k+\epsilon)]).
$$

Then:

$$
q=
\operatorname{CrossAttention}
(q_0,\{u_k\}).
$$

Finally:

$$
q
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\Delta t\\
\Delta R\\
p_{\rm effect}
\end{cases}
$$

and

$$
(\Delta t,\Delta R)
\xrightarrow{FK}
\Delta P_o.
$$

No residual flow initially.

Externally:

$$
z_k^{Cm}
=
W_{Cm}z_k.
$$

That becomes the representation you later test in the residual actor.

---

# What I would *not* change

I would keep all of these:

$$
\boxed{30\text{ Hz},\ stride=1}
$$

$$
\boxed{K=32}
$$

$$
\boxed{d_{\rm sweep}<20\text{ mm}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{hard threshold}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{swept current-action geometry}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{structured SE(3) output}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{tokens not being the sole bottleneck}}.
$$

Those are good decisions in the current branch.

### Priority order

If you want the minimum set of changes before serious training, I would do exactly four:

1. **Use token anchors/normals/mass in the effect head instead of `tokens.mean()`.**
2. **Replace independent token-over-points attention with competitive point→token assignment.**
3. **Turn off the current constant residual-flow head.**
4. **Add direct SE(3) supervision.**

Those four changes address the main structural weaknesses without disturbing your hard 2 cm swept-contact definition. After that, I think the architecture is strong enough to justify a real GRAB/DexYCB training experiment.
