I looked through the latest changes and the V1.7 partial training results. My conclusion is fairly clear:

> **Do not continue the current V1.7 residual training recipe. The main failure is not yet OI-Cm, and it is not evidence that residual RL is unsuitable. The residual policy is being initialized and explored in a way that immediately destroys an already competent DExplore base.**

The data are quite strong. The zero-residual gate had mean lift about **7.98 cm**, max lift **21.2 cm**, fingertip-object distance **4.9 cm**, contact occupancy **0.481**, and end-step success fraction **0.75**. By epoch 165 of V1.7, lift was **−54.6 cm**, fingertip distance **1.05 m**, contact occupancy **0.166**, and success was zero.

More importantly, this degradation starts immediately. Residual RMS is already **0.731 at epoch 1**, and reaches **0.852 at epoch 165**. The saved policies still have Gaussian standard deviation around **1.0**. In other words, PPO never operates near

$$
\delta a\approx0.
$$

It spends training around

$$
\|\delta a\|\sim O(1).
$$

That is the wrong regime for residual fine-tuning of a strong base.

The PPO configuration explains why: it has a fixed sigma initialized at zero in its parameterization, while the observed policy standard deviation is approximately 1.0; the residual policy itself is randomly initialized.

Given the physical residual scales,

$$
15\text{ mm translation},\qquad
0.20\text{ rad rotation},\qquad
0.08\text{ rad fingers},
$$

this means the hand is receiving roughly centimeter-scale wrist noise, \(\sim 10^\circ\)-scale rotation noise, and several-degree finger perturbations **from the first rollout**, every control step.

That is enough to destroy contact.

## There are two additional design problems worth fixing now

The first is that the residual policy currently outputs **18 dimensions**, but only 12 of those have physical meaning.

`compose_physical_residual()` actually uses:

$$
3\text{ translation}
+
3\text{ rotation}
+
6\text{ independent fingers}.
$$

The remaining six finger/mimic dimensions from the 18-D residual action are not used to generate independent physical corrections; mimic joints are reconstructed afterwards.

So PPO is solving an unnecessarily larger action-space problem:

$$
\boxed{18D\ residual\ policy\rightarrow12D\ effective\ control}.
$$

I would change the residual policy back to **12 dimensions**.

The second issue is subtler. The current action mapper has a discontinuity around zero residual. Exact zero residual uses the original DExplore target unchanged:

$$
a_{\rm final}=a_{\rm base}.
$$

But as soon as the residual becomes nonzero, the combined targets go through an absolute joint-limit clamp.

Therefore

$$
\lim_{\delta a\rightarrow0}
a_{\rm final}(\delta a)
$$

is not guaranteed to equal the exact zero-residual behavior if DExplore has targets outside the nominal clamp. For residual learning around a strong base, I want the composition to be smooth:

$$
\boxed{
a_{\rm final}
=
a_{\rm base}
+
\text{bounded residual}
}
$$

without changing some unrelated part of the base merely because \(\delta a\neq0\).

The recorded saturation ratio is only about 0.95%, so this is clearly not the main explanation for the catastrophic V1.7 result, but it should be fixed while we are touching the residual action contract.

## What I recommend next

I would make the next version a **residual-stability experiment**, not another Cm experiment.

1. **Make the residual actor 12-D.** Use only wrist translation 3 + wrist rotation 3 + six independent fingers. Expand to the 18-D native target internally through the mimic mapping.

2. **Initialize the policy at exactly zero residual.** Zero-initialize the final mean head:

   $$
   \mu_{\delta a}(s)=0
   $$

   at initialization. The deterministic initial policy must therefore reproduce the validated DExplore baseline.

3. **Reduce exploration variance by roughly an order of magnitude.** I would start with an effective normalized

   $$
   \sigma\approx0.05\text{--}0.10,
   $$

   not \(\sigma\approx1\). At \(\sigma=0.1\), typical exploration corresponds to only about

   $$
   1.5\text{ mm},\quad0.02\text{ rad},\quad0.008\text{ rad},
   $$

   which is much more appropriate for local correction. If the config parameter is log-standard-deviation, this means roughly \(-3.0\) to \(-2.3\), but I would verify that convention in the installed `rl_games` implementation rather than blindly changing the YAML.

4. **Keep OI-Cm out of the first corrected residual run.** Train

   $$
   \text{DExplore}+\text{safe residual PPO}
   $$

   first. If that cannot preserve or improve the base, Cm is irrelevant. Once it works, run the same setup with frozen Cm.

5. **Do short same-protocol gates, not another 1000-epoch run.** Under the *same* GPU, env count, reset protocol and reference, evaluate:

   $$
   \text{DExplore zero residual},
   $$

   $$
   \text{DExplore + residual PPO without Cm},
   $$

   and then

   $$
   \text{DExplore + residual PPO + Cm}.
   $$

   Use deterministic mean-action evaluation every 10–20 epochs. If contact/lift collapse relative to zero residual, stop immediately.

6. **Only after residual PPO is stable, test Cm first in the critic.** I would make the first Cm comparison:

   $$
   \pi(s)\quad\text{same actor observation}
   $$

   but

   $$
   V(s,C_m)
   $$

   versus

   $$
   V(s).
   $$

   If Cm improves value estimation/sample efficiency, then expose a compact Cm/effect feature to the actor. Do not combine “new residual exploration” and “new Cm-conditioned actor” in the same causal experiment.

I would leave the reward alone for this first corrected run. The current reward is simple—lift, fingertip distance, and a small action penalty.  It may eventually need improvement, and the `0.01` residual penalty is too weak to function as a strong trust region, but changing reward and exploration simultaneously would again make the result uninterpretable.

### The important conceptual correction

What we are doing is **not ordinary RL from scratch**.

DExplore already gives us a useful policy:

$$
\pi_{\rm base}.
$$

Residual PPO should begin in a tiny neighborhood around it:

$$
\boxed{
\pi=
\pi_{\rm base}
+
\epsilon,\qquad
\epsilon\approx0
}
$$

and gradually discover where deviation is beneficial.

V1.7 instead effectively did:

$$
\pi=
\pi_{\rm base}
+
\text{large stochastic disturbance}.
$$

The fact that performance became dramatically worse is therefore almost expected.

I would **not modify OI-Cm now**. The V1.7 result tells us virtually nothing about whether Cm helps, because the residual controller never established a stable local-learning regime. The immediate task is to prove:

$$
\boxed{
\text{residual PPO can start at DExplore performance and improve without first destroying it}.
}
$$

Once that passes, the Cm/no-Cm comparison becomes scientifically meaningful.



Because DExplore’s **policy interface is aligned with the simulator’s full 18-DOF Inspire state**, not with the hand’s minimal independent-control coordinates.

The released Inspire config sets `numDof: 18`.  And the Inspire task explicitly documents those 18 DOFs as

$$
6\ \text{wrist} + 12\ \text{finger}.
$$

But the crucial detail is what happens next: DExplore does **not** treat all 12 finger outputs as independent physical controls. In `_action_to_pd_targets()`, it overwrites six finger targets using fixed coupling rules:

$$
q_7=1.05q_6,\quad
q_9=1.05q_8,\quad
q_{11}=1.05q_{10},
$$

$$
q_{13}=1.05q_{12},\quad
q_{16}=0.6q_{15},\quad
q_{17}=0.8q_{15}.
$$

So the effective physical control rank is

$$
6\text{ wrist}+6\text{ independent fingers}
=
\boxed{12}.
$$

That means I should refine what I said earlier:

> **Using an 18-D residual is not itself the reason our training collapsed.**

It is redundant, but DExplore itself successfully trains with that redundant 18-D policy interface.

### Why would they deliberately keep 18 dimensions?

Mostly because it simplifies the whole RL/simulator interface.

The observation, DOF state, PD target, action dimension, and robot-specific environment all have the same native layout:

$$
a\in\mathbb R^{18}
\rightarrow
q_{\rm PD}\in\mathbb R^{18}.
$$

Then the Inspire-specific task layer enforces the coupling immediately before applying the PD targets.

This is convenient because their framework supports several different robot hands. The generic RL machinery doesn't need a special reduced action-space abstraction for Inspire; the robot-specific environment simply maps the generic native DOF vector into physically valid Inspire targets.

It also makes reference tracking easier because the reference data are represented in the same native DOF ordering.

---

## Then why can DExplore tolerate the six useless dimensions?

Because their values are overwritten before they affect physics.

For example, whatever the policy outputs in action coordinate 7, DExplore eventually does

$$
q_7^{PD}=1.05q_6^{PD}.
$$

Therefore that policy coordinate has essentially no causal effect on the environment.

That is inefficient, but not catastrophic.

PPO is optimizing in an 18-D nominal action space, but the environment effectively projects it onto a 12-D manifold:

$$
\boxed{
a_{18}
\xrightarrow{\text{coupling}}
a_{\rm physical}^{12}.
}
$$

This is conceptually similar to having six null directions in the action space.

---

# So why did our residual PPO deteriorate while DExplore didn't?

This is the more important question.

The answer is **not 18D versus 12D**.

The difference is that DExplore trained its **entire policy** under this action parameterization from the beginning, whereas we already have a strong pretrained base and are adding a stochastic perturbation to it.

Our V1.7 residual starts with normalized RMS around

$$
0.73
$$

at epoch 1 and policy standard deviation close to

$$
1.0.
$$

Only the effective 12 directions matter physically, but those 12 directions are still getting very large noise.

Given our physical residual scales,

$$
15\text{ mm},\quad0.20\text{ rad},\quad0.08\text{ rad},
$$

that is enough to immediately disturb a grasp that already works.

So think of the difference this way.

### DExplore

Starts from

$$
\pi_{\theta}^{random}
$$

and learns a complete manipulation policy while being allowed to explore widely.

There is no good controller to protect at initialization.

### Our residual learning

Starts from

$$
\pi_{\rm DExplore}^{good}
$$

and ideally should learn

$$
\pi
=
\pi_{\rm DExplore}
+
\epsilon,
\qquad
\epsilon\approx0.
$$

But currently we initialize closer to

$$
\pi
=
\pi_{\rm DExplore}
+
\epsilon,
\qquad
\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1).
$$

That's why the base behavior gets destroyed before PPO has learned anything useful.

---

## There is another important difference: DExplore's action is the policy itself

For DExplore,

$$
a_t=\pi_\theta(s_t).
$$

Its exploration distribution and reward landscape were designed around those actions.

For us,

$$
a_t
=
a_t^{base}
+
\delta a_t.
$$

The correct prior for \(\delta a_t\) is therefore not the same as the prior for a complete action.

A sensible prior is:

$$
p(\delta a)
$$

strongly concentrated around zero.

This is the mathematical reason I keep emphasizing zero initialization and small sigma.

---

# Should we change our residual to 12D?

I would still recommend it, but now for **efficiency and clarity**, not because 18D is inherently wrong.

There are two valid choices.

### Option A — keep 18D exactly like DExplore

Then do:

$$
\delta a_{18}
\rightarrow
\text{physical target mapping}
\rightarrow
\text{enforce mimic coupling}.
$$

Advantages:

* easiest compatibility with DExplore;
* same tensor/action layout;
* fewer code changes.

Disadvantage:

* six policy dimensions have no independent physical meaning.

This is completely acceptable for the next experiment.

### Option B — use the true 12D residual

Define:

$$
\delta a=
[
\delta p_w^3,
\delta R_w^3,
\delta q_{\rm independent}^6
].
$$

Then deterministically expand it to 18 native targets.

Advantages:

* no null action dimensions;
* cleaner policy interpretation;
* easier to inspect residual statistics;
* eventually cleaner for Cm-based residual learning.

I prefer this architecture long term.

But **I would not make 12D conversion the first fix now**. The critical fix is exploration magnitude.

---

## In fact, the current code already behaves partly like 12D

Our `compose_physical_residual()` receives an 18-D tensor, but it only uses:

* indices `0:3` for translation;
* `3:6` for wrist rotation;
* six `INDEPENDENT_NATIVE` finger indices.

The other residual finger entries do not become independent physical corrections. Then mimic joints are reconstructed from the independent values.

So changing the actor output from 18 to 12 mainly removes six redundant policy outputs.

It will not solve the current collapse by itself.

---

# Why did DExplore use large exploration successfully?

Because DExplore was not trying to preserve a pretrained controller.

It also uses dense reference-scoped rewards—hand tracking, object tracking, interaction geometry, contact terms—and very large parallel training with 8192 environments. The Inspire config has high interaction/contact weights such as `ig: 20`, `cg1: 5`, `cg2: 5`.

Our current residual reward is much simpler:

$$
20\,\text{lift}
-
2\,d_{\rm fingertip}
-
0.01\|\delta a\|^2.
$$

So DExplore can afford broad exploration because its reward tells the policy how to reconstruct a useful manipulation from scratch.

Our residual policy should instead exploit the fact that a useful manipulation already exists.

---

## Therefore I would revise the priority I gave you

The next change should be:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{keep 18D for compatibility if convenient, but initialize residual }\mu=0\textbf{ and make }\sigma\ll1.
}
$$

Then see whether:

$$
\text{DExplore}+\text{small residual}
$$

starts at approximately the DExplore baseline and improves gradually.

If that works, then switching to 12D is a sensible cleanup/optimization.

If it still fails with small residual exploration, then we investigate reward design and residual timing.

So the fact that DExplore uses 18D **does not contradict the diagnosis**. What matters is that DExplore's 18D is a full-policy interface projected onto a coupled 12D physical manifold, while our residual should be a **small perturbation around an already competent policy**.
